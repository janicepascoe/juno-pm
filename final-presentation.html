Juno, the PI Objectives Tracker

Juno is an AI assistant for product managers that monitors PI work against objectives and Key Results, surfaces evidence of material drift early, and recommends where the team may need to respond to protect intended Business Value.

What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno is an AI used by the product management team. Juno's goal is to monitor work against the PI objectives within a PI to determine if work is drifting away from objectives and if objectives should be updated or work should be changed.

Task

Monitor PI objectives during a PI defined for a quarter. Each run, it will create a simple and concise report of progress for the team. Do not make available beyond the team.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

Not filled in yet

What it demonstrates

Juno demonstrates one core flow: it reviews PI objectives and supporting evidence across ADO, Teams, and SharePoint, then produces a concise objective-by-objective report that identifies status, evidence, risks, alignment gaps, and decisions requiring attention without changing source systems.

Debrief

The prototype demonstrated that Juno can synthesize PI objectives and supporting evidence into a consistent objective-level status report while keeping recommendations separate from official decisions. It also exposed the importance of source quality, measurable Key Results, and explicit human approval boundaries. Because classroom constraints prevented connection to production ADO and SharePoint data, the prototype validates the workflow and interaction model, not production accuracy or reliability.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |

Justification

Juno may act without additional approval when the action is:

Read-only
Limited to information authorized for the current planning run
Reversible
Clearly labeled as a recommendation
Within the stated PI timeframe
Not an organizational commitment
Without asking, Juno may:

Analyze and synthesize authorized planning inputs
Draft and refine the status of the PI objectives
Propose recommendations for response to Key Results, metrics, targets, and data sources
Label assumptions and measurement gaps
Identify risks, dependencies, conflicts, and leadership decisions
Recommend candidate commitment status
Perform up to three draft-and-validation attempts
Produce a provisional result when gaps do not prevent responsible analysis
Juno must request authorization or surface a decision before it:

Commits, approves, assigns, publishes, sends, or changes official records
Changes or Assigns Committed Business Value, ownership, funding, or capacity
Changes an approved roadmap or priority
Accesses a source not authorized for the current run
Resolves a material strategic, capacity, clinical, governance, security, or compliance conflict
Saves information for use after the run
When approval is required, Juno must explain:

The decision or permission needed
Why it is needed
The available options
The impact of each option
Juno's recommendation, when evidence supports one

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Pillar is to monitor PI objectives each day, running each morning on it's own. It will create a summary but ask questions based on the information it retrieved for additional information or to keep it moving in sync.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno can create a draft summary and make that available to team members (not executives). The team may update data Juno can access to improve accuracy. Juno mostly reads ADO. Juno may create and refine its draft without permission. Any changes to ADO, SharePoint, or other source systems require PM approval.

Access Control:

For ADO, Juno should only look at the repositories selected for this product.
For the PI Objectives, Juno can look in the SharePoint for the project and read any data on the SharePoint for "Team Members". It cannot change data on the SharePoint.

Fallback Protocols:

Try accessing SharePoint 3 times. If it fails, alert the PM and stop. Try accessing ADO 3 times. If it fails, alert the PM and stop. If Juno is spending more than 15 minutes overall, alert the PM and stop.

Checkpoints:

If Juno spends more than 10 minutes attempting to review data from a single source, Juno should stop and ask the PM.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s PI Objective Status Review. Review the PI work against objectives and key results to give an early indication of where the team may need to respond to protect intended Business Value. Juno will highlight work that does not align to an existing objective. The result is a summary for each objective which contains suggested percent complete and a recommendation of potential new or updated objective for PM consideration.

Pass bar

An average of 4 or higher on prioritization, usefulness and alignment scores. as well as every yes or no question is scored a yes.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Accuracy: any "no"
- Private data: any "no"
- Any unsupported material alignment claim, tool access failure, and anything determined to be a hallucination.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Keep team focused on objectives throughout the sprints for early notification when we stray from objectives so we can correct as a team.

Baseline

Initial outcome baseline: PI completion rate 70%; official BV 8/10. Alignment-detection baseline will be established during the first PI using Juno.

Target and review date

Target: ≥80% of material objective-alignment issues identified by Juno are reviewed by the PM within five business days, with the resulting decision documented. Review: at the end of each sprint and formally at the close of the Program Increment.

Usage metric

Juno completes a status review each business day during the PI, and the PM reviews ≥80% of flagged material alignment issues within five business days.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

It is not causing different behavior in the team (no new or adjusted stories. PI objectives should flex to meet changing needs.

Stop line

Stop and reassess Juno if, for two consecutive sprints, <50% of its material alignment flags are judged actionable by the PM or ≥10% are determined to be unsupported by the cited evidence.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Conceptually, coming up with a very specific idea for a project was difficult. The ideas that I had require connection to my internal data that I cannot share in a classroom setting. I changed my product a couple times so I had to go back and redo some things.

Key learnings

There were a lot of details around how to keep an AI safe and effective that was new to me. For example, skills files are new to me. I intend to go back to these steps as a process when I want to make more complex AI solutions and when the harnesses are especially important.

Aha moment

I found myself constantly challenging my own openness to using AI. The examples and teachings encourage more complex AI solutions that I had not considered. My AHA moment is that I have been limiting my use of AI and need to think "bigger".
